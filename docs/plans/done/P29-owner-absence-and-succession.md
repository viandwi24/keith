# P29 — Owner absence and succession

Nov 10, 2026 · status: **done Nov 10, 2026** (Haiku implementers, orchestrated by Sonnet; `bun run check` 3896 pass and `bun run smoke` 74 steps pass) · depends on: P27 (presence and signals), P28 (protocol engine) · ADR: 0022 §3 and §4, 0024 §4, 0027 §6, §26 · closes: ADR-0022 §3 (heir, sign of life, check-ins, grace, claim, audit `owner.succession`), F-013 follow-up (ownership can pass only to a person who can sign in, now also on the succession path)

## Goal

If the owner is gone, Keith does not fall into the wrong hands and does not stay orphaned. The owner names one heir in advance; Keith notices the
silence, checks in on every channel, starts a visible grace period, tells both people, and lets the heir claim ownership with their own
credential and a step-up once the grace has ended. Any sign of life from the owner stops it at any moment. The previous owner stays in Keith as a
suspended member, and none of their secrets is ever shown.

## Why

- ADR-0022 §3 defines the `owner-absence` protocol (day 1 check-ins, day 14 default with a range of 7 to 90, 7-day grace, claim, audit `owner.succession`).
  ADR-0022 §2 says succession is the only way ownership moves without the owner acting, and §4 keeps `keith-core token recover` as the offline path.
- F-001 and F-013 (`docs/flow-review/2026-11-09-full/REPORT.md`): a delegate could take over the owner account. P21 closed that (delegation acts on members
  only, `transferOwner` needs a target that can sign in). This plan adds the intended path, so nobody ever needs a workaround.
- ADR-0024 §3: only the built-in succession protocol may change ownership, through its own coded path, never through an `act` step.
- ADR-0027 §6: audit rows about a person (here the previous owner) are visible to them through `audit.mine`.
- P22 roadmap: this plan is the owner-absence part (was M5).

## Scope

In:

- Heir setting (owner only, step-up, audited), succession window setting (7 to 90 days, default 14), pause for long trips.
- Sign of life: what counts, how it is read from P27 presence and signals, what does not count.
- Built-in `owner-absence` protocol on the P28 engine (disabled until an heir is named), small extensions to the P28 contracts that only built-ins may use.
- Succession state (one row), the heir's claim (own credential, step-up, after the grace, over a credential session only), the transfer that suspends the previous owner.
- Notifications to owner and heir on every channel, audit `owner.*`, brain events through the engine, doctor line, TUI (`Succession` section for the owner, `/succession` and a Home notice for the heir), CLI `keith-core succession status`.
- Tests that prove the claim path cannot be reached early, by the wrong person, or over the local socket.

Out:

- Several heirs, a quorum of members, a legal hand-over document (ADR-0022 revisit).
- Automatic transfer: nothing moves without the heir's claim (ADR-0022 §3).
- Recovery by mail, SMS or a recovery contact (ADR-0027 §29, not planned). Offline break-glass stays `keith-core token recover` (ADR-0008).
- Giving the heir access to the previous owner's private data beyond what an owner always has (see "What the heir gets").
- Mobile push (it will be one more channel of the delivery service of P25 when it exists).

## Design

### Settings and state

Settings (`settings` table, keys read through the existing settings repo; changes need `succession.manage`, a step-up and are audited):

| Key | Values | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `succession.heir` | a person id of an active member who can sign in; empty = none | none |
| `succession.windowDays` | integer 7 to 90 | 14 |
| `succession.pausedUntil` | epoch ms, at most 180 days ahead | none |

The grace period is fixed at 7 days (ADR-0022 §3). The protocol is off while `succession.heir` is empty.

New table `succession_state` (one row, `id = 1`, additive migration generated by `bun run --filter @keith/db db:generate`, backed up before it runs, ADR-0027 §3):
`state` (`idle`, `check-in`, `grace`, `claimable`, `claimed`), `run_id` (the P28 run), `heir` (person id at the moment the run started), `started_at`, `grace_started_at`, `claimable_at`, `last_sign_at` (the sign of life that was read when the run started), `updated_at`. The row is the claim's guard: the claim runs in one transaction that checks this row, the owner's last sign and the heir, then calls the transfer.

New capability `succession.manage` (owner only: added to `CAPABILITY_IDS` and `OWNER_ONLY_CAPABILITIES` in `packages/contracts/src/capabilities.ts`, `permissions.md`). It keeps a `people.manage` delegate (the spouse who helps with accounts) from naming themselves heir; the service also checks the owner role (same pattern as `notOwner`, ADR-0022 §1), so every path is covered.

### Sign of life

A **sign** is one of: an authenticated user action of the owner on any client (P27 signal `activity`: a message, an approval answer, an admin change, a TUI key press that P27 reports as `client.active`), a presence signal of the owner's own devices (`client.connected` after a sign-in, `device.location` when it exists; the actor of every signal is its person, so no separate "owner-made" flag exists), or an answered check-in (`protocols.answer` on the `ask` of this protocol). Not signs: passive polling (the same list `isUserActivity` in `apps/core/src/modules/auth/local-activity.ts` excludes), a node heartbeat, an automatic reconnect, any action by another person on the owner's account (a delegate cannot act on the owner, P21), `keith-core token recover` or any offline command (the host's break-glass is not the owner being present; it does reset the clock on purpose, see Risks).
Reading: `lastSign(personId)`, the port that P27 exports (owned by P27's spec "Ports"; this plan adds no second tracker and no port of its own); it counts signals whose `person_id` is the owner. Sign of life of the owner is `max(lastSign(owner), succession.pausedUntil)`, so a pause counts as a sign until it ends, and the count restarts from there.

### The protocol (`owner-absence`, built-in, `owner: keith`)

A definition generated by `modules/hypothalamus/builtins-succession.ts` from the settings, saved as a new protocol version whenever a setting changes (the version row is the audit of the change). Shape (steps are generated; the loops are explicit):

1. Trigger `absence { person: owner, afterMs: 24 h }`.
2. `limits.cancelWhen = signal.sinceRunStart(owner)` (new condition, below): any sign after the run started ends the run as `cancelled`, in every state including `claimable`. `limits.onCancel = notify-back`. `limits.maxSteps = 600`, `limits.maxVisits = 100`.
3. **Check-in loop**, one pass per day: `notify` to the owner on `channels: 'all'` ("Keith has not heard from you for N days. Reply 'I am here' or just use Keith; in D days ownership can pass to <heir display name>.") then `ask` to the owner (option `I am here`, `timeoutMs` 24 h, branch `timeout` goes to the next pass, branch `answer:I am here` ends the run `done`). After the last pass of the window a `branch` with `condition: not(signal.since(owner, windowDays))` goes to step 4. The pass count equals `windowDays - 1`, generated into the ladder; a settings change creates a new version and the next run uses it (a running run keeps its own version).
4. `system succession.begin-grace` (new step kind, built-ins only): sets `succession_state` to `grace`, writes `grace_started_at`, and audit `owner.succession.start`.
5. `notify` to the heir on `channels: 'all'` ("The owner of this Keith has been silent for N days. A grace period of 7 days started. If the owner does not return, you may claim ownership after <date>. Nothing happens before then.") and `notify` to the owner on `channels: 'all'` ("Ownership will move to <heir> on <date> unless you use Keith. Any sign from you cancels it.").
6. `wait 7 days` (daily reminder to the owner is a `notify` inside a `wait 1 d` loop, 7 passes).
7. `system succession.open-claim`: state `claimable`, audit `owner.succession.claimable`; `notify` to the heir ("You may claim now: /succession claim") and to the owner.
8. Weekly `wait 7 d` loop with `notify` to the heir until claimed or cancelled (bounded by `maxVisits`; after 52 weeks the run ends `expired`, state `idle`, owner and heir told).
9. `onCancel` (`notify-back`): `system succession.cancel` (state `idle`, audit `owner.succession.cancel` with the reason and the sign that cancelled), `notify` to the heir ("The owner is back; the succession protocol stopped.") and to the owner.

Texts are fixed strings; names are resolved by the delivery layer from ids at send time (the engine never interpolates data into text, P28 rule). The protocol runs as `keith` (self layer): it makes no model turn (`wake` is not used), so no prompt can be injected into it and no budget is spent.

#### Extensions to the P28 contracts (only built-ins may use the new parts)

- `StepSchema` gains `system { action: 'succession.begin-grace' | 'succession.open-claim' | 'succession.cancel' }`. `authoring.ts` rejects any `system` step unless the definition's `built_in` is `owner-absence`; the engine dispatches it to the succession service, not to a plugin or a node. This is the "own coded path" of ADR-0024 §3.
- `notify` gains `channels: 'best' | 'all'` (default `best`), mapped to the P25 `needs.fanout` field (`all` = every route the planner returns: every connected client of the person, queued clients for their next connection, and the front session). P25 already has `fanout`, so no edit of P25 is needed.
- `ConditionSchema` gains `signal.sinceRunStart { person }`; `LimitsSchema` gains `onCancel` (a step id run once when the run is cancelled by `cancelWhen`, before it ends).
- These land in P29.2.1, with their tests, and in `docs/specs/hypothalamus.md` in the same commit.

### The claim

`admin.succession.claim` (oRPC, capability `authenticated`, `stepUp: true`; the handler, not the gate, decides):

1. Refused unless the caller is the stored heir (`succession_state.heir` and the setting still equal the caller) and the session came from a credential (`via` is not `local`: the local socket serves the implicit owner, `auth.md`; a claim over it is `FORBIDDEN`). The caller is a member with `status = active` and a password or PIN (re-checked, F-013).
2. `succession_state.state = claimable` and `now >= grace_started_at + 7 days`. Anything else is `PRECONDITION_FAILED` with the reason (`not-started`, `grace-not-over`, `cancelled`).
3. The step-up of the caller's own PIN or password (`stepUpDenial`, `apps/core/src/modules/auth/capabilities.ts`; failures count in the same `auth_attempts` limiter, so a thief of the heir's bearer cannot guess).
4. Inside one SQLite transaction: re-read the owner's last sign (`lastSign`); if it is newer than `started_at` the claim is refused (`owner-returned`) and the run is cancelled. Otherwise `PeopleService.succeed(heirId, proof)` runs (below), `succession_state` becomes `claimed`, the setting `succession.heir` is cleared, the `owner-absence` protocol is disabled, the P28 run ends `done`.
5. Audit `owner.succession` (actor: the heir, `via`, detail: `previousOwner`, `heir`, `startedAt`, `graceStartedAt`, `claimedAt`, `lastSignAt`), written in the same transaction scope as the transfer, so there is no ownership change without the row (the audit writer never throws, ADR-0027 §7 style: the service checks the insert succeeded before it commits the transfer, else it rolls back).
6. Both people lose their sessions (the same `revokeSessions(…, 'transfer', …)` as a hand-over); the heir signs in again as owner.

`PeopleService.succeed(heirId, proof)` (new, `modules/people/people.ts`) is **not** an RPC procedure and not reachable from the CLI: `proof` is an opaque object only `modules/hypothalamus/succession.ts` can construct. It reuses the store call of `transferOwner` (`storeOf(by).transferOwner`), adds `status: suspended` for the previous owner in the same transaction, and keeps `transferOwner` unchanged. `transferOwner`'s existing checks (`callerIsOwner`, target has a secret and is active, target is not already owner) stay for hand-over.

### What the heir gets (and does not)

- The heir becomes owner with all owner rights over Keith's self layer (nodes, plugins, settings, providers, budget, protocols of `keith`).
- The previous owner remains a person, role `member`, `status: suspended`, so they cannot sign in until the new owner reactivates them (or hands ownership back). Their conversations, memory, workspace and resources are not deleted and not chown'ed.
- **Never revealed:** their password and PIN hashes (no API returns them), their credentials (revoked), their sealed provider keys that Keith holds (the new owner is the owner of Keith's settings, and provider keys are Keith's, not the person's; keys are never displayed by any API, ADR-0014), and anything in their personal layer beyond what the owner role already reads. The new owner reaches the previous owner's personal data only the way any owner reaches a member's: audited as `acl.owner-access`, visible to the previous owner through `audit.mine` once they can sign in (ADR-0015, ADR-0027 §6). Personal secrets stored as `person_secrets` stay hashes. The migration tests prove `people.get` and `admin.people.*` never return a secret field.
- The heir's own conversations, memory and agenda stay theirs.

### Audit, events, errors

Audit kinds: `owner.heir.set`, `owner.heir.clear`, `owner.succession.window` (days changed), `owner.succession.pause`, `owner.succession.resume`, `owner.succession.start`, `owner.succession.claimable`, `owner.succession.cancel`, `owner.succession` (the claim; the name the ADR fixes). Rows name the people by id; `audit.mine` shows the owner and the heir the rows where they are the subject (ADR-0027 §6). The engine's own `protocol.*` rows and `brain.hypothalamus.*` events (P28) cover each step; a succession start also emits `brain.hypothalamus.fire` with the protocol name.
Errors: `FORBIDDEN` (not the heir, not the owner, local socket), `PRECONDITION_FAILED` with `reason` (`not-started`, `grace-not-over`, `cancelled`, `owner-returned`, `heir-invalid`), `BAD_REQUEST` (heir is the owner, suspended, cannot sign in, window out of range, pause too long), `CONFLICT` (a claim already processed).

### RPC, CLI, TUI

RPC (`packages/contracts/src/contract.ts`, handlers `modules/hypothalamus/succession-procedures.ts`): `admin.succession.get` (owner: heir, window, state, last sign, paused; heir: only `{ role: 'heir', state, claimableAt }`; anyone else `NOT_FOUND`), `admin.succession.setHeir` (`succession.manage`, step-up), `admin.succession.clearHeir`, `admin.succession.setWindow`, `admin.succession.pause` (`until`), `admin.succession.resume`, `admin.succession.claim`.
CLI: `keith-core succession status|heir <person>|heir --clear|window <days>|pause <days>|resume` (owner; mutating ones need the running core and a step-up like other admin changes; `status` also runs offline), `keith succession claim` for the heir (a credential session). `keith-core doctor` prints `succession: off`, `armed (heir: <name>, window 14 days)`, or `grace until <date>`.
TUI: new Manage section `Succession` (`apps/tui/src/features/manage/succession/`, `read: ['succession.manage']`, in the `People & access` group of `features/home/menu-state.ts`) with the state, last sign, an `heir` picker (active members who can sign in), `window`, `pause for a trip` (days, max 180) and `resume`. Copy: `Heir: Ana  ·  window 14 days  ·  last seen 2h ago  ·  armed`, `Paused until 2026-12-03 (trip)`. The heir sees, only during `grace` or `claimable`, a Home caption `Ownership claim: open from 2026-11-24 · /succession`, and the slash command `/succession` shows the dates and, when `claimable`, `claim` behind a step-up dialog with the typed word `claim`. The owner, on any client, sees the cancel notice and a `Succession cancelled` toast.

## Risks and mitigations

- **A false succession takes the account of a living owner.** Layers: owner-only heir setting with a step-up; 14 days of silence by default (min 7); daily check-ins on every channel; 7 days of grace with daily reminders; any sign cancels at every state; the claim re-reads the last sign inside the transaction; the claim needs the heir's own credential plus a step-up. Nothing is automatic.
- **The heir's account is compromised.** The claim still needs a step-up (PIN or password, rate limited with the existing `auth_attempts`), and the heir was told when the grace started; a stolen device-login bearer alone cannot claim. The owner can clear the heir at any time (a sign of life as well).
- **A delegate names themselves heir.** `succession.manage` is owner only and the service checks the role; tested with a `people.manage` delegate.
- **Break-glass resets the count.** `keith-core token recover` is the host's authority, not the owner's presence; it does not count as a sign, but whoever runs it controls the machine and can already change ownership offline (ADR-0022 §4). Documented, not mitigated.
- **A long trip.** Pause up to 180 days, audited, visible in the TUI; without a pause the owner gets check-ins and answers one with a single key.
- **Check-in fatigue or a missed channel.** `channels: 'all'` plus queued delivery; a client that connects later shows the queued check-in, and using it counts as a sign.
- **Testing real days.** Tests and smoke use the injected clock of the engine (P28 tests); no test sleeps. No time-scale setting or env switch is added to production code; the manual walk-through moves stored timestamps in the test database with `manual:clock` (P29.3.6).
- **Dependency drift.** `lastSign` (P27) and delivery `fanout: 'all'` (P25) come from those plans; steps P29.2.1 and P29.2.2 begin by reading their specs and fix a gap in the owning spec first.

## Gate evidence (Nov 10, 2026)

- Commits 18282d1 (spec) to 87fe538 (documentation); `bun run check`: 3896 pass, 0 fail, check-imports, check-size, check-exports (19 entries) ok. `bun run smoke`: SMOKE PASS, 74 steps (block `scripts/smoke/succession.ts`: the real core, the stored times shifted back 1, 8 and 7 days, check-in, grace, claimable, a claim without a step-up refused, the claim with the heir's step-up, the previous owner suspended, one `owner.succession` audit row).
- Migration: core `0018` (`succession_state`, one row by `CHECK id = 1`), generated, on top of the P26 backup.
- **Ladder (deviation, decided).** The protocol `owner-absence` checks in with one `ask` a day on every channel (`channels: 'all'`, the P28 `ask` gained that field), in weekly blocks of seven asks, with exits by silence thresholds. A `notify` plus a one-day `wait` a day would cost 178 executions for 90 days and end the run `failed` near day 50 (the 100 step cap): the owner would be silently unprotected. Every window from 7 to 90 days gives an enabled valid definition (6 passes for 7 days, 13 for 14, 89 for 90; 96 executions at 90 days).
- **Enabled rule (decided).** `owner-absence` is enabled if and only if an heir is set. The owner cannot switch it off, pause, save or delete it through the protocol procedures (`use keith-core succession`).
- **Claim phase is reconciled, not run (decided).** The run ends `done` after `open-claim`; `reconcileClaimable()` runs on status, claim, boot and each protocol tick while the state is `claimable`: an owner sign newer than the claim opening returns the state to `idle` (reason `owner-sign`), and 52 weeks without a claim expire it. The claim re-reads the owner's last sign inside its transaction. The weekly claim reminder is not built (a non-goal: the heir is told once at the claim opening).
- **Catch-up.** A core that was down 9 days reaches grace on its first tick, both notices once; down 16 days: grace at once and claimable 7 days after the restart (not backdated: the owner must get the grace notice before a claim can open).
- **Claim.** Heir only, over the heir's own credential with a fresh step-up, refused on the local socket, in one transaction (swap, previous owner suspended, state `claimed`, heir cleared, `owner.succession` audit). The sessions of BOTH people are revoked (like transfer-owner), so the new owner signs in again: the TUI signs out like `/logout` after a claim. After a claim `owner-absence` follows the new owner (no heir: disabled).
- Other decisions: a pause for a trip sends no notice to the heir (it is the owner's audited choice, and telling the heir the owner is away is presence information); the heir view carries only the state and `claimOpensAt` (no owner name, no presence); `status` over the CLI needs a running core (`doctor` is the offline view); `succession.manage` is owner only.
- Deviations recorded by the implementers: `PeopleService.succeed` takes an opaque proof type that only the succession module can build (a test pins its importers); a run id is a digest of protocol id and trigger ref; the cancelled heir is told through the cancel audit row, not a notification; the toast width (40 cells) splits the claim success text into two toasts.
- Fixed by the orchestrator after the agent reports: `manual:clock` did not move `protocol_versions.saved_at`, which the absence trigger counts silence from, so the timeline never started; the smoke kept the owner's first TUI connected, whose re-registration after each restart is a sign of life that rightly cancels the run (the smoke now stops it before the block); the smoke asked the admin CLI as the previous owner after the claim (the claim revokes those sessions); the guest kill smoke step raced the 2 s listener poll (it now accepts the port closing and waits for the reopen); four Manage tests counted 13 sections and forbade every admin call from a member (the heir view call is open to every person and answers NOT_FOUND for a non-heir); a stale `device.location` line in the spec and a "(planned)" marker in ARCHITECTURE.
